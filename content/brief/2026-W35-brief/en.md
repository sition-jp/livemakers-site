# LiveMakers Weekly Brief — W35 / Aug 24 - Aug 30 2026

**He Never Said "Hike" — One Sentence at Jackson Hole Repriced Gold and Crypto, and Left Equities Alone**

## Executive Summary

The week's impulse came from a lodge in Wyoming on Friday morning. In "In Our Time," his first Jackson Hole address as Chair, Kevin Warsh did not announce a rate increase. He said the opposite: "I stand here today committed to a discipline, not to a decision." The strongest line in the text is this one — "Here is my standard: We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do."

We have work to do. On that sentence, the market-implied probability of a September hike moved from 35% the day before to **59%** (CME fed funds futures). Prediction markets were more cautious — Kalshi at 48%, Polymarket at 49% — leaving a ten-point gap between futures and event markets reading the same speech.

**What matters is that the assets which repriced and the assets which did not split cleanly.** Gold fell 2.60% on the week and broke hard on Friday. Bitcoin went from $79,438 at 14:00 UTC, the hour of the speech, to $77,346 at 22:00 — down 2.6% in eight hours. The dollar firmed (DXY +0.89%) and USD/JPY closed back at 160.04. Equities barely moved: the S&P 500 finished the week **+0.49%**, the Nasdaq Composite +0.85%, and the VIX fell to 14.43.

In Issue #1 (W30) we described a chain that ran energy → rates → equities and stopped just before crypto. This week is its mirror image. **A monetary-policy shock moved the dollar, gold and crypto, and stopped just before equities.** In five weeks, the same market changed where the circuit breaks.

There is a second, structural event worth recording. This was the symposium's 49th year, and its theme — "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy" — put payments and tokenization at the organizing center for the first time in its history. Darrell Duffie of Stanford presented "Tokenized finance and the perimeter of central banking," with Isabel Schnabel of the ECB Executive Board as discussant. Her response, "Central banks on-chain," was unambiguous: "Stablecoins cannot replace central bank reserves as the economy's ultimate settlement asset." **Tokenization took the agenda. It did not take the keynote.** In Warsh's text, the word "token" appears only in its AI sense — the unit you buy to access a model.

### 1.2 What the speech said, and what the market read

Read against the primary text, the speech has three parts: long-horizon questions about AI as a new factor of production; forward guidance and the relationship between the central bank and markets; and the policy principles Warsh applies to the economy as he sees it. On guidance he was pointed — "You can call it an outline . . . you can call it a trail map . . . just don't call it forward guidance." The author, in other words, opened by declaring that he was not making a forecast.

Markets moved anyway, because the assessment section was concrete. Warsh said he is "impressed by the overall performance of the economy, which appears to have strengthened," and stacked the evidence: four-quarter growth in equipment and intangibles investment "around 9 percent, its highest growth rate since 2021"; S&P 500 profits up more than 20% over the past year; credit spreads on corporate bonds and leveraged loans "near the low ends of their historical ranges"; and July's Senior Loan Officer survey showing commercial and industrial lending standards "on the easier end of their historical range." His conclusion: "on balance, I would be hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive."

Unemployment, at 4.1%, "remains low by historical standards." On the target, he was categorical: "The Fed's price-stability objective of 2 percent, as measured by the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, is a firm, fixed target." **Strong economy, easy financial conditions, high inflation — assemble those three and the conclusion follows without being stated.** That is what the market read.

There was a second input that morning. The Bureau of Economic Analysis released July personal income and outlays before the speech: the PCE price index rose 3.7% from a year earlier and 0.2% on the month; core PCE rose 3.3% year-over-year and 0.2% on the month, both in line with expectations. Personal income rose 0.4%, consumer spending 0.2%. **The confirmation that inflation had not fallen landed a few hours before the Chair said "we have work to do."** In that order, it was enough.

### 1.3 Currency and fiscal regime — the intervention decayed in four weeks

Reading this week's USD/JPY as a rate-differential story misses the more important part.

On August 1, the US Treasury bought yen alongside the Japanese authorities. It was the first US intervention in support of the yen since the 1998 Asian financial crisis, and the first joint US-Japan yen-buying operation in twenty-eight years. The method was unorthodox: the New York Fed, acting for the Treasury, sold euros rather than dollars to buy yen — reportedly to avoid a path in which Japan, the largest foreign holder of US government debt, would fund unilateral intervention by selling Treasuries.

USD/JPY was 163.86 on July 28, before the operation. It reached 157.53 on August 3. It closed at 160.038 on August 28. **That is the first close at or above 160 since July 30 — since the correction the intervention produced.** Four weeks later, essentially the whole move has been given back.

Japanese long yields rose in the same week: the 10-year to 2.897% and the 30-year to 4.038%, each up 4.3bp. The US-Japan 10-year spread actually narrowed slightly, from 1.884% to 1.823%, and the yen weakened regardless. Something beyond the differential is at work, and it involves the market's assessment of fiscal management. Markets are reported to be pricing a high-80s percent probability that the Bank of Japan raises to 1.25% in September (a reported figure, not one we measured directly from futures). **A currency that does not recover even as tightening is priced in has moved out of the rates question and into the credibility question.**

Europe pointed the same way. Schnabel said this week that "at the current policy rate, inflation is unlikely to return to target over the medium term, and therefore further tightening will be necessary." Euro-area inflation accelerated to 2.9% in July, and she flagged European gas prices as particularly worrying given low storage. The ECB Governing Council meets September 9-10; the FOMC on September 15-16. **September arrives with all three major central banks holding tightening back on the table.**

### 1.4 Oil went the other way

It is easy to miss, but crude fell this week: Brent -6.46%, WTI -4.16%. Five weeks after the supply shock that took Brent to $100 in Issue #1, the price is back in the $88 range. The confrontation around the Strait of Hormuz continues — US Central Command said it had cleared Iranian-placed sea mines from the strait, while Iran maintains the strait stays closed until its terms are met.

**The fact that prices fell anyway carries more information than the rhetoric.** A supply risk premium does not accumulate automatically with the length of a disruption; it decays when the market concludes that alternative routes and inventories are working. This week's decline is also evidence about the character of the inflation problem: it is not currently coming from a supply shock. It is coming from domestic demand and easy financial conditions — the same picture Warsh described when he said financial conditions are hard to call restrictive.

### 2.2 Flows were the strongest in ten months. Price fell anyway

The most informative fact in this lane is the gap between flows and price.

US spot bitcoin ETFs had been taking in money since August 17. The week through August 21 drew **$1.92 billion in net inflows, the largest weekly total since October 2025.** The run continued into this week: $337.6M on August 24 and $242.3M on August 27, making **nine consecutive sessions of inflows — the longest streak since April.** August inflows reached $3.03 billion through August 27, leaving the month $390 million short of October 2025's total with four sessions to go. Spot ether ETFs matched the pattern with $235M on August 27, also a ninth straight session; spot Solana ETFs added $60.91M and spot Hyperliquid ETFs $24.42M.

BTC still finished the week down 1.20%. The intraday path explains it: bitcoin peaked at $80,591 at 01:00 UTC on August 28, then stepped down through the afternoon and evening — $78,371 at 16:00, $77,897 at 18:00, $77,528 at 20:00, $77,346 at 22:00. **High to low, -4.03% on the day. One speech offset nine sessions of ETF accumulation inside a single session.**

The reading is that ETF flows are not a sufficient condition for price. In Issue #1 we noted that 69.6% of a $727M inflow stretch went into a single product, and called that "not diffuse capital responding to macro." This week supplies the sequel. **Even with flows back at ten-month highs, the asset sold on a change in the expected rate path.** What changed is the composition of the buyers, not the character of the asset.

### 2.3 Why Solana went the other way

SOL was the only major to finish up double digits. Two things converged.

On the technical side, mainnet slot time fell from 400 milliseconds to 350 at the start of epoch 1020 on August 21 — **the first reduction since the network began producing blocks in 2020.** The change, specified in SIMD-0525, shipped in the Agave v4.2 validator client. The plan is four 50ms steps toward a 200ms target; the next stop is 300ms, with no activation date set. Note that this is a separate axis from Alpenglow, the consensus overhaul aimed at cutting finality from roughly 12.8 seconds toward about 150 milliseconds.

On the flow side, weekly inflows into US spot Solana ETFs were the largest of 2026, with roughly $33.5 million arriving on August 25 alone. Cumulative net inflows into those products now exceed $1.1 billion.

The ETF flows, however, are small relative to the increase in SOL's market value over the week. **The size of the announced technical change and the size of the price response are not proportionate.** The more accurate reading is not that the catalyst produced the move, but that this was a week in which capital gathered where there was a catalyst. Consistent with that, SOL sold off with everything else after Friday's speech.

### 2.4 Policy — the Senate calendar hardened around September

The digital asset market structure bill (the CLARITY Act) is where it was on August 8, when the Senate Majority Leader filed the motion to proceed. The filing came too late for a vote before the August recess, but it sets up a procedural vote almost immediately after the Senate returns. The open items are government ethics provisions, law enforcement provisions, and the treatment of stablecoin yield and rewards.

Nothing substantive has moved in the text since the July 22 revised draft covered in Issue #1. **What changed is that the date on which votes get counted went from "sometime" to "September"** — the same month that now holds both the FOMC and the ECB Governing Council.

### 3.2 The growth happened on single days

The daily series for WisdomTree and Ethena USDtb shows that this was not gradual accumulation. WisdomTree sat flat in the $778 million range from August 21 through August 26, then **jumped to $1,029.1 million in one day on August 27.** Ethena USDtb did the same thing a day earlier, moving from $334.7 million on August 25 to **$484.5 million on August 26.**

That is not the market buying more of an asset. That is an issuer adding a distribution channel. In WisdomTree's case, a corporate cash-management platform announced on August 25 that it had added two of the firm's tokenized funds — a short-duration income fund and a floating-rate Treasury fund — to its terminal. **The day the balance moved and the day the connection was added are the same day.**

In Issue #1 we cited Spiko adding roughly 93% to its on-chain balance in a week and wrote: "That is distribution expanding, not beta." This week the same phenomenon appears at different issuers on an even shorter clock. **RWA balances are not a sentiment gauge for demand. They are a gauge of wiring.** Five weeks apart, that reading has now reproduced.

### 3.3 18.5% of the aggregate is simply the gold price

This week the structure showed itself from another angle.

Two of the largest decliners are Tether Gold (-$116.2M) and Paxos Gold (-$65.4M). Including the smaller products, tokenized gold totals $5.106 billion — **18.5% of the entire RWA aggregate.** The size of this week's decline is almost fully explained by the fall in the gold price (-2.60%). The daily balance series for both protocols shows a distinct step down at the final August 28 observation, coincident with Friday's move in gold.

Which means: **when the Fed Chair speaks about inflation and gold is sold, the number called "on-chain RWA balances" mechanically shrinks.** It shrinks even if no holder redeems anything. This is not a defect in the aggregation; it is what the aggregation is defined to measure. But anyone who receives it as the one-line claim "the RWA market grew / contracted this week" will misread it.

This week's +1.38% (+$376M) is the residual of three unrelated events: roughly $650M of new wiring arriving, roughly $180M carried away by the gold price, and $147M of USYC redemptions. **Demand, price and distribution are all inside the same single number.**

### 3.5 The institutional side — the week tokenization made the agenda

The real story in this lane is neither price nor balances.

The Jackson Hole symposium is in its 49th year, and the 2026 theme was "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." **This is the first time in those 49 editions that digital payments and financial technology sat at the organizing center.** Six research papers and three panels covered payments, tokenized assets and banking, with roughly 120 participants from more than 70 countries.

At the center was Darrell Duffie's paper, "Tokenized finance and the perimeter of central banking." Its argument starts from the observation that wholesale tokenization — representing assets and money as tokens on programmable ledgers — **has been held back by the absence of a safe settlement asset.**

The discussant was Isabel Schnabel of the ECB, and her response, "Central banks on-chain," answered the question directly.

> "Stablecoins cannot replace central bank reserves as the economy's ultimate settlement asset."
>
> "No matter how advanced the technology or how deep the liquidity of private tokens, financial markets can only scale safely if transactions settle in a risk-free asset that can be supplied elastically."
>
> "Central banks should go on-chain too. That means bringing central bank money into the tokenised environment."

A stablecoin issuer, in her framing, "has no independent capacity to meet the second criterion: the ability to expand liquidity elastically." **This is not a rejection; it is a redrawing of the perimeter.** She grants the usefulness of tokenization and reserves only the asset at its base for the central bank.

## 4. Cross-Lane Signals

### 4.1 Where the chain stopped — the mirror image of Issue #1

In Issue #1 the causal chain ran energy → inflation expectations → rates (+13.8bp) → equities (-2.13%), and stopped before crypto. We concluded then that "this was not crypto withstanding macro; it was capital operating on a separate clock."

This week the chain ran the other way. Policy stance → hike odds (35% → 59%) → dollar (+0.89%) → gold (-2.60%) and crypto (BTC -2.6% in the eight hours after the speech) — and **stopped before equities.** The S&P 500 rose 0.49% on the week, the Nasdaq Composite 0.85%, and the VIX fell to 14.43. Germany's DAX set a record high after the speech.

The same market changed where the circuit breaks, five weeks apart. Two things follow.

**First, the proposition that crypto is independent of macro was falsified in five weeks.** July's non-reaction was not evidence of independence; it reflected the character of that week's flows. This week, with ETF money arriving at its heaviest in ten months, the asset repriced straightforwardly on the rate path. If anything, it behaved more like a traditional asset than it did in July.

**Second, the harder thing to explain is equities.** A 24-point jump in hike probability accompanied by an equity rally is not self-evident. It reconciles against the text: Warsh repeatedly described a strong economy, and the facts he offered as grounds for tightening — 9% capex growth, 20%+ profit growth, low credit spreads — are not bearish for equities. **The same speech was read by the rates market as a tightening signal and by the equity market as a confirmation of growth.** Neither reading contradicts the document.

### 4.2 Gold now sits in two lanes at once

For the first time we can observe the macro lane and the RWA lane connecting through a single asset.

Gold is a macro asset. It is also, through tokenized gold, 18.5% of the on-chain RWA aggregate. Therefore — **when the Fed Chair's remarks move the price of gold, they automatically move the number reported as "the size of the RWA market."** This week that path was live.

This bears directly on how the indicator should be read. If RWA balances are being used to track the progress of tokenization, the gold contribution has to be separated out, or progress and price movement get confused. Conversely, seeing the aggregate shrink in a week gold fell and concluding that "tokenization lost momentum" is simply an error. **If we are going to run these as separate lanes, the components that straddle them have to be handled explicitly.** We will separate this out in each issue going forward.

### 4.3 It took the agenda. It did not take the keynote

The third crossing is institutional.

The theme was payments and tokenization. Duffie's paper took the perimeter of central banking itself as its subject, and Schnabel argued that central bank money should be brought on-chain. **On that evidence alone, tokenization has entered the mainstream agenda of central bank policy** — for the first time in 49 years.

The Chair's keynote did not go there. The subject Warsh chose under the heading of financial innovation was AI. He framed generative AI as "potentially a new factor of production" and cited the figure that "annualized token sales for the two leading labs alone" exceed $100 billion, "an increase of 500-plus percent from a year ago." Not payments, not tokenization, not stablecoins. The only place digital assets appear in the text is a footnote citation to his own 2022 essay.

**The asymmetry has a practical implication.** Being recognized as a research agenda and entering the priority order of policy operations are different things. The question Duffie and Schnabel put on the table — who supplies the safe settlement asset — carries into the next phase without an implementation decision attached. Meanwhile the AI capex cycle is already a variable the Chair uses when assessing financial conditions. **Tokenization entered the agenda; AI entered the judgment.** That is the order to take away from this year's symposium.

### 4.4 Satellite: the AI capital cycle

This lane was source-backed from more than one direction this week.

From the podium, Warsh treated AI as "potentially a new factor of production" and observed that "more than half of the capex growth this year can likely be ascribed to the buildout related to AI." His open questions were whether AI produces a sustained rise in economy-wide productivity, whether token usage is complementary or competitive to labor, and whether the next model generation demands still greater capital intensity. **A central bank has explicitly written AI investment into the cyclical picture.**

From the industry, Nvidia reported fiscal 2027 second-quarter results on August 26: revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year-over-year and 18% sequentially; Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117% year-over-year and ahead of the $86.33 billion consensus; and guidance of $108 billion (±2%) for the current quarter against expectations of $104.2 billion. CEO Jensen Huang: "AI has reached its inflection point. It's doing useful work. Its tokens are productive and profitable. Now, compute is revenue. And demand is accelerating."

**In the same week the Chair asked whether token usage complements labor, the supply side answered that compute is revenue.** Note, though, that the stock extended its decline in after-hours trading following the release. What is being priced is not the print but the second derivative of the growth rate — the same measure Warsh said he would keep watching.

## 6. Next Week

1. **US employment situation (Friday September 4, 8:30 ET)** — Warsh called the 4.1% unemployment rate "low by historical standards" and does not treat the labor market as a constraint on tightening. Whether that assessment survives is the main branch point ahead of the September 15-16 FOMC. Weakness in the labor data is the fastest route back for this week's repricing.

2. **ECB Governing Council (September 9-10)** — Schnabel has said further tightening will be necessary, and euro-area inflation accelerated to 2.9% in July. The Council as a whole is reported to have little appetite to signal beyond one move. **Watch the guidance more than the decision.**

3. **Does the equity non-reaction hold** — the central unresolved question. Whether the S&P 500 and the VIX can stay here with a 59% hike probability priced. Gold and crypto have already moved; either equities follow with a lag, or the rates market walks its pricing back. That choice sets the character of next week.

4. **The ETF streak** — whether nine consecutive sessions of inflows (the longest since April) survives Friday's decline. If it breaks, that also settles whether August's total reaches the October 2025 record. **Watch which way the flow-price divergence resolves.**

5. **The CLARITY procedural vote** — scheduled for shortly after the Senate returns. The unresolved treatment of stablecoin yield and rewards is, in practical terms, the other face of the question Schnabel posed: who supplies the settlement asset.

**Watch list (not covered above, tracked forward)**: tokenized equities balances (xStocks at $432M, +1.83% over seven days) and their price-reference mechanics / the activation timing of Solana's next 300ms step / real-economy pass-through of the US-Canada trade friction / Japanese fiscal management and super-long JGB supply-demand / expansion of private-markets secondaries.
